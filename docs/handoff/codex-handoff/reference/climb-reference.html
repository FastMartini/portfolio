    01Context

    The problem

    The team explored how an international investor might discover a U.S. market move, inspect the catalyst, and act without losing sight of what happened next.

    The concept joined a momentum scanner, a trade ticket, and portfolio feedback in one coherent demonstration. Its job was to make the path from signal to decision legible—not to promise investment performance or access in any particular country.

    02Frame

    Users and constraints

    Audience

    Curious first-time investors and active traders

    The demo followed one fictional user from demo dollars to a scanner-led trade.

    Market constraint

    The market was closed during the build

    A minute-by-minute replay kept prices, signals, news, and trades on one clock.

    Execution constraint

    No real securities changed hands

    The team used mock stock tokens and dUSD on Solana devnet.

    Delivery constraint

    A weekend and one demo laptop

    The must-have loop took priority over live hosting, backtesting, and short selling.

    Key decisions

    1. 01

      Replay one real market day.

    2. 02

    4. 04

      Make signing explicit.

      The backend built an unsigned swap, Phantom signed it, and the vault co-signed and submitted it—an inspectable sequence instead of a hidden transaction.

    The problem

    Political articles can shape interpretation through source reputation, loaded language, and selective framing even when the question is not simply whether a sentence is true or false.

    Veritas was designed for readers who want a quick, structured second look while they remain on the article page. The extension surfaces article metadata, ranked claim candidates, and an estimated leaning breakdown so the presentation is easier to inspect.

    Readers of online political news

    The interaction starts from the active Chrome tab and keeps the explanation inside the extension popup.

    Extraction constraint

    Article markup is inconsistent

    The extension searches semantic page containers and visible paragraphs, so poorly structured pages can reduce input quality.

    Model constraint

    The signals are estimates

    Confidence and bias values come from model output; they are not objective ground truth or a measured accuracy score.

    Product boundary

    Context, not a verdict on truth

    The system estimates presentation patterns and deliberately does not fact-check the article.

    04Engineering

    Key decisions

    1. 01

      Narrow the product to political leaning.

      The team moved away from generic misinformation classification and framed the output as an estimate of editorial presentation.

    2. 02

    Waypoint 03Privacy research

    Membership Inference Attack Study

    A classical machine-learning privacy study asking whether model behavior can reveal which text samples appeared in training.

    I led the testing and evaluation work across the target classifier and learned membership attack.

    As team lead, I coordinated the project and led the design and execution of testing procedures alongside Christian Cevallos, Lucas Ramos, and Justin Cardenas.

    View source repository Read the research report Watch public demo
    Experiment
    500 member and 500 non-member reviews
    My role
    Team lead · Testing and evaluation
    Format
    Four-person research team

    01Context

    The problem

    A classifier can keep its training reviews hidden and still reveal clues about which reviews it has seen.

    Membership inference predicts whether a particular sample was used to train a model. It does not need to recover the original text: knowing that a record was included can itself reveal sensitive information. Our question was whether the behavior of a sentiment classifier exposed enough signal to distinguish training reviews from unseen reviews.

    This was a classical machine-learning experiment: TF-IDF represented the text, and logistic regression classified sentiment. A second logistic-regression model learned to predict membership from the target classifier’s outputs.

    02Frame

    Experiment design

    Dataset

    Two distinct IMDB subsets

    We sampled 500 members from IMDB’s official training split and 500 non-members from its official test split. Sampling was stratified by sentiment with random seed 42; the target trained only on members.

    Target model

    Words to sentiment

    TF-IDF used up to 5,000 terms and removed English stopwords. Its vocabulary was fitted only on member reviews, then applied to unseen reviews. Logistic regression predicted positive or negative sentiment.

    Baseline attack

    A single confidence threshold

    The midpoint of member and non-member mean maximum confidence defined the threshold. Reviews at or above it were predicted to be members. The baseline was evaluated on all 1,000 reviews used to derive that threshold.

    Learned attack

    A separate membership classifier

    The 1,000 attack-feature rows were split 70/30, stratified by membership with seed 42: 700 for attack training and 300 for evaluation. The held-out set contained 150 members and 150 non-members of the target model.

    03Public evidence

    Inspect the experiment.

    The report records the experiment’s measurements and team contributions. The code exposes how the data, target model, and attacks were constructed; the recorded demo provides a walkthrough.

    Follow the public materials

    Dataset preparation establishes which reviews the target sees. Target training fits the text representation and sentiment classifier. Attack implementation defines the signals, threshold, learned model, and evaluation split.

    Watch the recorded public demo linked on the report’s cover, or follow the repository’s run instructions to inspect the pipeline.

    The measurements below reproduce the report’s recorded run. They are not a new experiment or a claim about every text classifier.

    04Engineering

    Key decisions

    1. 01

      Keep membership grounded in the training boundary.

      Only the member subset trained the target classifier and TF-IDF vocabulary. Non-member reviews remained unseen by that training process, preserving the meaning of the membership labels.

    2. 02

      Start with an inspectable baseline.

      A confidence threshold made the initial question easy to test. The learned attack then combined five behavior signals rather than relying on a single score.

    3. 03

      Separate sentiment performance from attack performance.

      The target predicts sentiment; the attacker predicts membership. I led testing and evaluation across both stages, keeping the evaluation task and sample group explicit when interpreting the results.

    4. 04

      Hold out rows for the learned attack.

      The attack model’s evaluation rows were excluded from its own training. A held-out attack row can still be a member of the target’s training set: these are two different training boundaries.

    05Signals

    Attack features

    • Python
    • scikit-learn
    • TF-IDF
    • Logistic regression
    Maximum confidence
    The largest predicted sentiment probability.
    True-class confidence
    The probability assigned to the review’s known sentiment label.
    Loss
    The negative log of true-class confidence; lower loss indicates a better fit to that review.
    Entropy
    The uncertainty across the predicted sentiment probabilities.
    Correctness
    Whether the predicted sentiment matches the known label.

    The learned attack needs probability outputs and the review’s true sentiment label to construct these features. It also trains on labeled membership examples from the controlled experiment. This access is an assumption of the study.

    Results

    The recorded run exposed a measurable membership signal in this classifier’s behavior.

    Target sentiment classifier · 1,000 reviews

    Non-members76.6%500 reviews unseen during target training

    These are sentiment accuracy measurements, not membership-attack success rates. The training-to-unseen gap is consistent with overfitting and motivates testing for privacy leakage; the gap alone is not proof of membership for an individual review.

    Membership prediction · reported attack metrics
    MetricBaseline threshold attackLearned attack
    Accuracy69.2%70.7%
    F169.0%72.3%
    ROC-AUCNot reported0.765

    The baseline threshold was derived from and evaluated on all 1,000 reviews. The learned attack trained on 700 rows and was evaluated on the remaining 300. Because the evaluation sets and fitting procedures differ, 69.2% and 70.7% are not a like-for-like estimate of improvement.

    08Boundary

    Limitations

    A controlled sample. The results cover 500 member and 500 non-member IMDB reviews, one target classifier, and one recorded run. They do not establish a general privacy guarantee or population-wide attack rate.

    Attacker access matters. The learned attack assumes probability outputs, known sentiment labels, and labeled membership training examples. A system that exposes less information presents a different attack setting.

    Different evaluation procedures. The baseline uses the same rows to derive and evaluate its threshold; the learned attack holds out 30%. A shared untouched evaluation set would support a stronger comparison.

    No uncertainty estimate. The report does not provide repeated runs, confidence intervals, or statistical significance tests. Small differences between methods should be interpreted cautiously.

    No mitigation evaluation. Differential privacy is discussed as related work but was not implemented or tested. This experiment measures leakage rather than establishing a defense.

    Scope of the evidence. The study uses classical text classification and public movie reviews. It neither extracts private text nor establishes how these findings transfer to other datasets or model families.
